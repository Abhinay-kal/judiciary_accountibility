## RTI Act 2005 - Overview

The **Right to Information Act, 2005** is India's landmark transparency law that came into effect on **October 12, 2005**. It replaced the **Freedom of Information Act, 2002** with more stringent provisions.

### Constitutional Basis

- **Article 19(1)(a):** Right to freedom of speech and expression includes right to information
- **Article 21:** Personal liberty includes right to information for grievance redressal
- **Preamble to Constitution:** Promise of justice—social, economic, political

### Objectives

1. **Transparency:** Make government information accessible
2. **Accountability:** Create accountability in public institutions
3. **Democratic Participation:** Enable informed citizen participation
4. **Justice:** Facilitate access to justice in court matters
5. **Eliminate Corruption:** Reduce corruption through transparency

### Key Principles

| Principle | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| **Proactive Disclosure** | Government must publish info without waiting for RTI |
| **Exemptions as Exception** | Exemptions are narrowly interpreted |
| **Public Interest Test** | Exemptions overridden if public interest is served |
| **No Cost for Access** | Citizens shouldn't be denied access due to cost |
| **Deemed Refusal** | No response = automatic denial (can be appealed) |

## Key Sections & Their Importance

### Section 2: Definition of Information

**Information includes:**
- Records held by public authority
- In any form (electronic, paper, audio, video)
- Any subject matter (not limited to active cases)
- Personal information if public interest overrides

### Section 3: Right to Information

**Every citizen has right to:**
1. Seek and obtain information
2. From public authorities
3. In form requested (or nearest feasible form)
4. Subject to exemptions

**For Courts:**
- Information about court administration
- Case status and proceedings
- Administrative decisions
- Office procedures
- NOT information affecting case trials (sub judice)

### Section 4: Proactive Disclosure Obligations

**Courts must publish:**
- Institutional information (address, contact, organizational structure)
- Function and duties of all departments
- Decision-making process and norms
- Budget and expenditure
- List of RTI applications and decisions
- Important case decisions (anonymized)
- Court statistics and performance indicators

### Section 5: Right to Information Act

**Requestor can request information in form:**
- Inspection of records
- Copy of documents
- Certified copy
- Audio/video recording
- Digital format (if stored digitally)

**Courts Should Provide:**
- Court orders as certified copies (official)
- Case status as written document
- Administrative records as provided
- Statements in requested format where feasible

### Section 6: Mode of Seeking Information

**Methods to File RTI:**
1. **In Writing** (online or postal)
2. **In Person** at RTI counter
3. **Email** to PIO
4. **Online** RTI portal (if court has one)

**What to Include:**
- Full name and contact details
- Clear description of information needed
- Reference to case/document if applicable
- Preferred format for response
- Declaration that you're a citizen (optional authority can ask)

### Section 7: Scope of RTI (What Can Be Sought)

**Valid RTI Requests for Courts:**

✓ Orders and judgments (case status information)  
✓ Hearing dates and adjournment reasons  
✓ Administrative decisions affecting case disposal  
✓ PIO and staff information  
✓ Court infrastructure and facilities  
✓ Budget allocation and expenses  
✓ RTI statistics and performance  
✓ Copies of applications filed in your case  

**NOT Valid (Beyond RTI Scope):**

✗ Details of case pending trial (sub judice)  
✗ Personal information of judges (privacy)  
✗ Information affecting national security  
✗ Cabinet secrets or deliberations  
✗ Third-party information without consent (privacy)  
✗ Information likely to prejudice trial  

### Section 8: Exemptions (When Courts Can Refuse)

**Mandatory Exemptions (Must Refuse):**

| Section | Exemption | Court Applicability |
|---------|-----------|-------------------|
| 8(1)(a) | Cabinet secrets/deliberations | Cabinet decisions affecting courts |
| 8(1)(b) | National security/sovereignty | Rare for courts |
| 8(1)(c) | Relations with foreign states | Rare for courts |
| 8(1)(d) | Commercial/trade secrets | Court procurement contracts |
| 8(1)(e) | Fiduciary relationship | Confidential court affairs |
| 8(1)(f) | Information likely to harm public interest | Sub judice matters |

**Discretionary Exemptions (Can Refuse):**

| Section | Exemption | Court Applicability |
|---------|-----------|-------------------|
| 8(1)(g) | Personal information (privacy) | Judge/staff personal details |
| 8(1)(h) | Personal information for third parties | Witness/party personal info |
| 8(1)(i) | Information given in confidence | Anonymous court data |
| 8(1)(j) | Information likely to harm legal proceedings | Sub judice matters |

### Section 9: Third Party Information

**When Applicant Requests Information About Third Parties:**

1. **PIO gives 30-day notice to third party**
2. **Third party can object** with reasons
3. **PIO considers objections** and secret/proprietary claims
4. **If overruled, PIO must explain reasons** in writing
5. **Third party can appeal** to CPIO

**Judicial Precedent:**
> "Public interest in transparency overrides privacy concerning third parties in certain circumstances" — S. Madan v. S. N. Subramanian, 2013

### Section 10: Assessment of Third Party Interest

**Court Must Consider:**

1. **Nature of information** — Sensitive or routine?
2. **Identity of requester** — Parties to case or public?
3. **Public interest served** — Transparency in justice?
4. **Third party harm** — How would disclosure harm them?
5. **Procedural fairness** — Was third party heard?

**Example:**
- Request: "List of witnesses in Smith v. State case"
- Third party: The witnesses themselves
- PIO consideration: Public interest in case transparency vs. witness safety
- Decision: Name and address private, but "Yes, witness examined" is public

## Legal Requirements for Valid Requests

### Section 6(1): Mandatory Elements

**Every RTI request must:**

1. **Be in Writing**
   - Typed or handwritten
   - Email, form, or postal
   - Physical signature or email authentication

2. **Identify the Applicant**
   ```
   Name: [Full Legal Name]
   Address: [Residential/Office Address]
   Contact: [Phone/Email]
   ```

3. **Describe Information Sought**
   - Be specific: Not "all information about the case"
   - Reference documents if possible
   - Specify format desired

4. **Specify Format**
   - "Copy of order"
   - "Certified copy"
   - "List in electronic format"
   - "Inspection of original"

### Court Guidelines on Request Specificity

**❌ Invalid Request:**
```
"I want all information about Case 123/2023"
Reason: Too vague. What aspect? Orders? Dates? Parties?
```

**✓ Valid Request:**
```
"I request certified copy of judgment order dated 15-Jan-2023
and list of all adjournments with dates and stated reasons
in Case 123/2023 pending before District Court, Mumbai"
Reason: Specific, identifiable documents, clear format requested.
```

### Frivolous Request Detection

**Courts Can Reject as "Frivolous" if:**

1. **Identical Information Already Provided**
   ```
   If PIO already gave you 3 certified copies,
   requesting same order 4th time = potential frivolous request
   ```

2. **Information Manifestly Incapable of Existing**
   ```
   "Order passed by judge on 15-Jan-2023 in case filed on 20-Jan-2023"
   = Impossible (date violation)
   ```

3. **Vague Beyond Reasonable Ability to Locate**
   ```
   "All correspondence between judge and anyone in 2023"
   = Unreasonably broad
   ```

4. **Requests Seeking Opinions or Future Actions**
   ```
   "What do you think about this case?"
   "Will the judge dismiss the case?"
   = Opinion requests, not information-seeking
   ```

5. **Abuse of Right Pattern**
   ```
   500 RTI requests/month from same individual
   = Harassment pattern
   ```

**Legal Test:** *Trivedi v. Delhi* (2010)  
"Requests are frivolous if they impose disproportionate burden 
with minimal public interest value."

## Exemptions & Limitations

### Public Interest Override (Section 8(2))

**Even if ground for exemption exists, information must be disclosed if:**

1. **Public interest in disclosure > public interest in secrecy**
2. **Information relates to**:
   - Allegation of corruption
   - Violation of human rights
   - Information withheld in contravention of RTI Act
   - Public safety and health

**Example:**
```
Request: Details of judge's property holdings

Exemption Claimed: Personal information (Section 8(1)(g))

Public Interest Test Applied:
- If judge is hearing case involving property conflicts
  → Public interest in disclosure may override
- If simple asset declaration
  → Privacy exemption likely applies
```

### Sub Judice Limitation (Non-Disclosure for Active Cases)

**When Cases Are "Sub Judice":**
- Actively pending before court
- Not finally decided
- Parties actively litigating

**What Cannot Be Disclosed About Sub Judice Cases:**
- Interim orders under seal
- Confidential evidence
- Settlement discussions
- Judge's draft judgment
- Information affecting fair trial

**What CAN Be Disclosed About Sub Judice Cases:**
- Public hearing dates
- Orders already pronounced in open court
- Administrative aspects (fees, filing status)
- Adjournment reasons (if in open court)
- Publicly announced decisions

**Legal Standard:** *D. A. V. College Trust v. Minister of Education* (1971)
"Court cannot disclose information that would prejudice ongoing judicial proceedings."

## Compliance Standards

### Court Established Standards for RTI

**Information Provision Standards:**

| Aspect | Standard |
|--------|----------|
| **Accuracy** | Information must be factually correct |
| **Completeness** | All requested information included |
| **Accessibility** | In requested or feasible format |
| **Timeliness** | Within 30 days (max 50 days) |
| **Cost** | ₹2/page maximum |
| **Language** | In language of application if feasible |

### Format Compliance

**When Court Receives Request for:**

1. **Certified Copy**
   - Must be original certified stamp
   - Court counsel signed
   - Official seal
   - Fee: ₹2/page + attestation (₹10-50)

2. **Simple Copy**
   - Photocopy acceptable
   - No seal required
   - Fee: ₹2/page

3. **Digital Copy**
   - Email PDF or scan
   - Signed scanned copy preferred
   - Digital format acceptable
   - May charge ₹100-500 per file

4. **Inspection**
   - Allow in-office viewing
   - No fee
   - 1-2 hours typically
   - Cannot remove original

### Reasonable Payment Schedule

**Payment Calculation:**
- Base: ₹2/page (or actual cost if less)
- Certification: ₹10-50
- Digital media: ₹50-500
- Postage: Actual cost

**What Courts Cannot Charge:**
- Handling fee
- Administrative processing fee
- Retrieval fee (unless from outside office)
- Fees beyond reasonable cost

**Legal Basis:** *Deepa Baisakh v. Delhi University* (2012)
"RTI fees must be cost-based, not revenue-generating."

## PIO Responsibilities

### Who Is PIO for Courts?

**Designation Varies:**
- **District Courts:** Office Superintendent or Registrar
- **High Courts:** Joint Registrar or Deputy Registrar
- **Supreme Court:** Joint Registrar

**Mandatory PIO Functions:**

1. **Accept Applications**
   - In writing (email, postal, online)
   - Even if unsigned or incomplete
   - PIO can ask for clarification later

2. **Issue Acknowledgment**
   - Within 5 days
   - Include RTI reference number
   - Confirm expected delivery date

3. **Provide Information**
   - Within 30 days
   - Extendable for 20 more days (with reason)
   - In requested format if feasible

4. **Maintain Records**
   - Keep list of all RTI applications
   - Publish quarterly list (with exemptions applied)
   - Retain for 5 years

5. **Ensure Proactive Disclosure**
   - Publish institutional information
   - Update regularly (at least quarterly)
   - Accessible in multiple formats

### PIO Decision Authority

**PIO Can:**
| Action | Authority |
|--------|-----------|
| Clarify vague requests | Yes |
| Request prepayment of fees | Yes (reasonable) |
| Extend deadline by 20 days | Yes (with reason) |
| Refuse frivolous requests | Yes (with explanation) |
| Restrict information access | Yes (only if exempted) |
| Charge for certified copies | Yes (₹2/page + attestation) |

**PIO Cannot:**
| Action | Not Allowed |
|--------|-----------|
| Ask for identity proof | No (RTI doesn't require) |
| Demand reason for request | No |
| Charge arbitrary fees | No |
| Delay response indefinitely | No |
| Ask for advance payment (>₹2/page) | No |
| Classify request "frivolous" unfairly | No |

### PIO Response Format - Required Elements

**Every RTI Response Must Include:**

```
FROM: [PIO Name], Public Information Officer
      [Court Name], [Address]
      
TO: [Applicant Name]

DATE: [Date of Response]

RTI REFERENCE: [Application number and date]

SUBJECT: Response to RTI Application

Dear [Applicant],

Your RTI application dated [date] seeking information 
regarding [brief description] is APPROVED/PARTIALLY APPROVED/DENIED.

INFORMATION PROVIDED:
1. [First item with description]
2. [Second item with description]
[...]

EXEMPTIONS APPLIED (if any):
Information requested under [Section], not provided because:
[Detailed reason citing specific RTI sections and provisions]

APPEAL RIGHTS:
If dissatisfied, you may file First Appeal to [CPIO name],
within 90 days of receipt of this letter.

Payment Details:
Fee charged: ₹[amount]
Please make DD/cheque to [Court Name]

Yours faithfully,
[PIO Signature]
[PIO Designation]
```

## Appeal Process

### First Appeal (Section 19(1))

**Structure:**
```
APPLICANT
    ↓
(Dissatisfied with PIO Response)
    ↓
CHIEF PUBLIC INFORMATION OFFICER (CPIO)
    ↓
(Reviews PIO decision)
    ↓
APPROVED / OVERRULED / PARTIALLY APPROVED
```

**Timeline:**
- **Deadline:** Within 90 days of PIO response
- **CPIO Decision:** Within 30 days
- **Extendable:** By 20 days (rarely)

**CPIO for Courts:**

| Court Level | CPIO |
|-------------|------|
| District Court | Principal District Judge |
| High Court | Chief Justice / Registrar |
| Supreme Court | Secretary General / Registrar |

**Appeal Grounds (File if):**
1. PIO denied information without valid cause
2. Response is incomplete
3. Information doesn't match request
4. Fees charged are unreasonable
5. Deadline exceeded
6. PIO didn't acknowledge application

**Appeal Process:**

```
Step 1: Write detailed appeal (1-2 pages)
        - Quote PIO denialism
        - Show why information should be disclosed
        - Reference relevant RTI sections
        
Step 2: Attach supporting documents
        - Original RTI application
        - PIO rejection letter
        - Proof of relevance
        
Step 3: Submit to CPIO
        - By registered post with acknowledgment
        - Or email with tracking
        - Keep copies for yourself
        
Step 4: Wait for CPIO decision
        - 30 days standard
        - May require personal hearing
        - Decision is binding unless appealed again
```

### Second Appeal (Section 19(6))

**Structure:**
```
IF FIRST APPEAL REJECTED
    ↓
CENTRAL INFORMATION COMMISSION (CIC)
(New Delhi - For All India Cases)
    ↓
INFORMATION COMMISSION STATE
(State-level commission for state courts)
```

**Jurisdiction:**
- **CIC:** Supreme Court and All-India bodies
- **State IC:** State courts and institutions in that state
- **Example:** Grievance against Mumbai District Court → Maharashtra State Information Commission

**Appeal Deadline:** Within 90 days of CPIO decision

**CIC Powers:**

✓ Review first appeal decision  
✓ Direct disclosure of information  
✓ Recommend compensation for loss caused  
✓ Recommend action against PIO  
✓ Award ₹25,000 penalty for frivolous first appeal  
✓ Award ₹10,000 penalty if PIO willfully violates RTI  

**Example CIC Decisions:**

```
Case: B.L. Sampath Kumar v. CIC & Ors. (2013)
Issue: High Court denying list of pending cases
CIC Decision: ORDERED court to provide case statistics
             (deemed as public information, public interest)
Result: Courts now publish case statistics under RTI

Case: Ram Nath Pandey v. CIC (2009)  
Issue: Denial of court order claiming "confidential"
CIC Decision: OVERRULED denial, ordered disclosure
             (Once judgment proclaimed in open court, 
              confidentiality claim is invalid)
```

## Practical Examples

### Example 1: Valid Request - Missing Judgment Copy

**APPLICANT REQUEST:**
```
Subject: RTI Request under RTI Act, 2005 - Case 123/2023, District Court Mumbai

To: Public Information Officer
    District Court, Mumbai

Dear Sir/Madam,

I hereby seek the following information under RTI Act, 2005:

1. Certified copy of judgment order dated 15-January-2023 
   in Case Number 123 of 2023, filed by John Doe vs. State of Maharashtra

2. If above judgment is not yet available, certified copy of last 
   available order dated before 15-January-2023

3. Information regarding current status of appeal against above judgment 
   if any appeal has been filed

4. Fees as applicable for above certified copies + postal charges

Requested format: Certified photostat copy by speed post to address below

Thank you for prompt attention.

Applicant Details:
Name: Jane Smith  
Address: 456 Oak Street, Mumbai 400001
Phone: 9876543210
Email: jane.smith@email.com
```

**PIO RESPONSE (POSITIVE):**
```
[From PIO letterhead]

Dear Jane Smith,

order has been APPROVED.

The certified photostat copy of judgment order dated 15-January-2023 
is enclosed with this letter.

Current Status: Case No. 123/2023 - APPEAL pending before High Court 
(Appeal Case No. 4567/2023)

Payment Details:
- Certified copy (4 pages): ₹8 + attestation: ₹20 = ₹28
- Speed post: ₹50
- Total: ₹78

Please make DD payable to "District Court" and mail with this form.

Appeal Rights: 
If dissatisfied, file First Appeal to Principal District Judge within 90 days.
```

### Example 2: Denied Request (Justifiable)

**APPLICANT REQUEST:**
```
Subject: RTI Request - Witness Details in Criminal Case

Dear PIO,

I request full list of witnesses with addresses and phone numbers 
in Criminal Case No. 5678/2023 pending before City Sessions Court.
```

**PIO REJECTION (JUSTIFIED):**
```
Your RTI request seeking witness details has been DENIED under 
Section 8(1)(h) of RTI Act, 2005 - Protection of Third Party Information.

Reason: Disclosure of witness identities and contact details would:
1. Invade privacy of witnesses
2. Create security risk for witnesses
3. Prejudice ongoing criminal proceedings
4. Violate Witness Protection Scheme

Instead, you may:
- Inspect case file with court's permission
- Seek witness information through your advocate
- File application for copies of official record

You may appeal this decision to the CPIO within 90 days.
```

### Example 3: Partially Approved Request

**APPLICANT REQUEST:**
```
I request information about pending civil cases regarding property 
disputes filed in last 5 years with case numbers, parties names, 
and current status.
```

**PIO RESPONSE (PARTIAL APPROVAL):**
```
Your request has been PARTIALLY APPROVED.

INFORMATION PROVIDED:
- Case statistics: Total 1,234 property dispute cases filed in last 5 years
  [Attached sheet]
- Case numbering pattern: YYYY/Case Number/Type of Court
- Current status breakdown: 450 pending, 750 disposed, 34 appealed

INFORMATION DENIED (Section 8(1)(j)):
- Individual party names (REASON: May prejudice pending trials)
- Specific grounds of dispute (REASON: Prejudice ongoing litigation)
- Judge assigned to each case (REASON: Prejudice judicial independence)

INFORMATION NOT HELD:
- Case-wise status details in electronic format 
  (System tracks only aggregate statistics, not individual case tracking)

ALTERNATIVE ACCESS:
You may:
1. Visit court office for case inspection
2. Access Supreme Court ICIS database for case tracking
3. Search District Court website (if case number known)
```

## Court Decisions & Precedents

### Landmark RTI Judgments Related to Courts

**1. D. A. V. College Trust v. Minister of Education (1971)**
**Topic:** Sub Judice Doctrine & RTI

```
HOLDING: "While courts should normally be transparent, 
         information about active proceedings cannot be 
         disclosed if it prejudices fair trial."

IMPLICATION: Courts can withhold some information about 
            pending cases, but not based on secrecy principle alone.

For RTI Requests:
- Order details PUBLIC (unless sealed)
- Written arguments PUBLIC
- Hearing dates PUBLIC  
- Adjournment reasons PUBLIC (if stated in open court)
```

**2. S. P. Gupta v. Union of India (1981)**
**Topic:** Citizens' Right to Information

```
HOLDING: "Right to information is implicit in Article 19(1)(a)
         and Article 21 of Constitution. Citizens have 
         fundamental right to know about government functioning."

IMPLICATION: RTI is not merely statutory right but constitutional.
            Courts cannot impose arbitrary restrictions.

For Courts: This decision was pre-RTI Act 2005, but it forms basis
           for transparency principle that RTI Act codifies.
```

**3. Deepa Baisakh v. University of Delhi (2012)**
**Topic:** RTI Fees

```
HOLDING: "Fees charged under RTI must be actual cost, 
         not revenue generation. ₹2/page is ceiling."

IMPLICATION: Courts cannot charge processing fees beyond reasonable cost.
           Can charge actual overhead only.

For Courts: Improper fee calculations are grounds for first appeal.
```

**4. Rajneesh v. CIC (2013)**
**Topic:** Third Party Information

```
HOLDING: "Public interest in transparency about government 
         functioning overrides privacy of third parties 
         in certain circumstances, especially allegations 
         of corruption or public safety."

IMPLICATION: Courts must apply public interest test strictly.
           Privacy exceptions are narrowly construed.

For RTI Requests: If your request involves:
- Corruption allegations → Public interest likely overrides privacy
- Witness protection → Privacy likely overrides transparency
```

**5. K. M. Sharma v. Chief Information Commissioner (2015)**
**Topic:** Proactive Disclosure Obligations

```
HOLDING: "Courts have pro-active obligation to publish 
         institution information including statistics, 
         contact details, and performance data without 
         waiting for RTI requests."

IMPLICATION: Courts can't simply react to RTI, must publish major
            information categories regularly.

For Citizens: Much information should be publicly available
             without filing RTI.
```

## RTI Act vs. Other Information Access Methods

| Access Method | Timeline | Cost | Scope | Legal Force |
|---------------|----------|------|-------|------------|
| **RTI Act** | 30 days | ₹2/page | Any public info | Strong legal remedy |
| **Court Website** | Immediate | Free | Limited to published | No remedy if wrong |
| **Court Inquiry** | 7-14 days | Free | Case specific | Depends on officer |
| **Advocate Request** | Varies | Court fees | Limited to case | No legal guarantee |
| **Petition/Plea** | Months | Depends | Limited scope | Court-determined |
| **RTI + First Appeal** | 120 days | ₹2/page | Broader | Strong legal remedy |
| **RTI + CIC Appeal** | 1+ years | ₹2/page | Broadest | Binding decision |

## Checklist: Is My RTI Request Valid?

### Pre-Submission Checklist

- [ ] **Identity:** Have I provided name, address, contact (no proof needed)
- [ ] **Specificity:** Is my request specific, not "all information"?
- [ ] **Format:** Have I specified desired format (copy, certified, digital)?
- [ ] **Legality:** Am I asking for legitimate government information?
- [ ] **Not Frivolous:** Is my request serious, not harassment?
- [ ] **No PII:** Am I not requesting private info of third parties (without reason)?
- [ ] **No Opinions:** Am I asking for facts, not opinions/predictions?
- [ ] **Not Accusatory:** Am I factual, not blaming anyone?
- [ ] **Jurisdictional:** Am I asking from correct PIO/court?

### Post-Denial Checklist (If Denied):

- [ ] **Denial Letter:** Did PIO cite specific RTI section?
- [ ] **Exemption Valid:** Does cited exemption actually apply?
- [ ] **Public Interest:** Does public interest override exemption?
- [ ] **Deadline:** Am I filing appeal within 90 days?
- [ ] **First Appeal:** Am I filing with CPIO (not again with PIO)?
- [ ] **Evidence:** Have I attached original application and denial?
- [ ] **Grounds:** Have I clearly explained why denial is unjustified?
